> Good but not great

## Why

- Interesting lane-based combat with different archetypes
- Giant space mechs will always be cool
- Story/characterisation is delivered in a variety of ways
- Whole other game included in the roguelike mode

## Why Not

- Voice acting is bland enough it kinda detracts from the experience
- Nothing to make it really stand out from similar games
- Gives you too much leeway; I never felt in danger of losing

## Impressions

Another review where I bemoan Steam's binary thumbs up/thumbs down; I definitely can't give a game this good a thumbs down but it also doesn't really do enough to set it apart from the absolute flood of deckbuilders.

One cool point of differentiation is that it's story-based, with no half-hearted attempt at roguelike elements to ride that wave (though there is a full-hearted separate roguelike mode, making this pretty great value for money). This was the main draw for me; if I want to play a deckbuilder endlessly Slay the Spire 2 is right there. Unfortunately the story joins more or less every aspect of the game in the 'well-made but unspectacular' bucket.

There are distinct and likable characters, but the overall plot is very much just a way to move from point A to point B. There's voice acting, but unfortunately the performances are bland enough that I started enjoying the story a lot more on mute, skipping through as fast as I could read, something I never even considered in [Inscryption](/games/reviews/2025/inscryption.md). I'm a sucker for deckbuilders which use lanes as a concept but this isn't as creative with them as something like [Cobalt Core](/games/reviews/2025/cobalt_core.md), and the game never pushed me enough to feel like the different combos on offer were necessary to really think about.

It sucks because the devs clearly put a lot into this game, and they succeeded in making a very competent story-based deckbuilder. But the other games I mentioned in comparison have stuff that set them apart and made them immediately come to mind when I looked for comparisons; I can't say Battle Suit Aces will ever do that for me. If you're looking for a deckbuilder with a story that ends (not that it has to after they added the roguelike mode for free, almost a year after launch) this is certainly one of the options, and you probably won't regret it. But you probably won't remember it in a year or two either, so given the endless sea of fantastic games clamouring for your time this is a tough recommend.
