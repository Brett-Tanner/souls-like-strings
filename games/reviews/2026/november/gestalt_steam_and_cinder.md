> WTF was that ending

## Why

- Old fashioned but well executed
- Cool backdrops and general aesthetic
- More -vania than metroid, which is less common
- Combat has a nice flow between melee/ranged

## Why Not

- If you wanna backtrack it can be painful
- Story is a bit incoherent, into a baffling ending

## Impressions

Gestalt is a pretty solid retro-feeling Metroidvania along the lines of [Momodora](/games/reviews/2026/april/momodora_reverie_under_the_moonlight.md), but set in a steampunk fantasy rather than pure fantasy. You play as a reheaded badass wielding a gun and sword which you'll often switch between as you mow down waves of decently varied enemies to level up & get better gear. There's also a story, which in a charming throwback is mainly delivered via cutscenes as you transition between major areas. Levelling up gives you skill points to allocate as you see fit, with a mix of '+x to stat n' and more meaninful upgrades like new moves or more shots in your revolver.

Overall it's solidly executed and enjoyable, not gonna blow your socks off like [Silksong](/games/reviews/2025/silksong.md) or [Nine Sols](/games/reviews/2026/october/nine_sols.md) but if you're looking for more Metroidvanies to play this is better than many others.

Unfortunately what most will remember about this game is its ending. Being mortally wounded by a cheap shot immediately after facetanking multiple giant sword attacks, energy slashes and void fireballs during the final boss fight you just won completely shattered the image built by the rest of the game. You feel so powerful by the end, the story constantly talks you up, and you just beat by far the hardest fight in the game. Then you get hit once by the smallest gun you've seen in the game and peace out, letting your 'guy in the chair' take center stage for the final cutscene for some reason...
