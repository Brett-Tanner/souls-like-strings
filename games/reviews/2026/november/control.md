> Worldbuilding so great it overshadows the main story

## Why

- You like the SCP Foundation universe
- Some spectacular vistas & visual presentation
- Solid combat and traversal
- Metroidvania-ish elements with the powerups

## Why Not

- The main story is kinda meh next to stuff you hear about in collectibles
- Enemies don't force you into different combat approaches

## Impressions

Like most Remedy games, Control feels like it could've been a TV show. Unlike most Remedy games t think that wouldn't have been a better format; this one is actually fun to play.

The story starts off strong as our protagonist, Jesse Faden, walks into the seeming abandoned Federal Bureau of Control building. She meets a janitor who seems to psychically summon an elevator for her, then the Director. Though technically she's a little late to 'meet' him since he just shot himself. She picks up his gun (apparently taking his title at the same time) and begins her journey to fight off the mysterious 'Hiss' who've corrupted the agency's staff, but mostly to rescue her long lost brother Dylan.

Through a 15-20 hours runtime (depending on how much you interact with side content/DLC) you'll learn a lot more about Jesse, her brother, the Bureau and the endless strange developments they've dealt with through the classified folders and recordings abandoned by hiss-ified staff. These scattered materials are one of the best parts of the game, drawing on SCP-like themes and story telling to keep you intruiged without revealing too much. The main story is also engaging as you move through some spectacular set pieces and discover more about the Faden siblings' respective pasts. It's the bits in between the plot and scattered collectibles which can fall short, as side content makes an attempt to deliver some additional lore but too often boils down to formulaic tasks.

You'll also gain a satisfying range of paranatural abilities like throwing stuff really hard, creating a debris shield to throw really hard and floating through the air to eventually throw yourself at the ground really hard. Your trusty stolen gun also acquires new forms, allowing you to play more like a sniper, up close and personal with a shotgun or take a variety of other approaches. These abilities/modifications combine with decent if not great enemy variety to keep things fresh over the runtime, though I wish they'd done more to force you into different approaches. I'm aware it's on me for not just making my own fun, but staying back and repeatedly hurling heavy objects while sniping started as and remained a safe and easy way through every encounter.

Overall a very entertaining experience with some great moments; excited to try Resonant one day since the combat is much more my style than this.
