> Good thing Yi has more than 9 lives

## Why

- Combat feels amazing once you learn it
- Surprisingly emotional story
- Fantastic boss designs & ramping difficulty
- Best Metroidvania/Souls-like combo I've played

## Why Not

- Exploration won't help much with difficulty; you need to git gud
- Biomes are only as different as 'high tech spaceship' allows

## Impressions

How do you make your mark in a decades-old genre that requires you to compete with _two_ Hollow Knights and a sea of games trying to be them? Nailing the basics helps, as does a compelling story and likable characters; but some of the best combat I've ever experienced is what clinched it for me.

In the beginning you might not share my opinion; I certainly didn't. Especially coming from other metroidvanias where your dash is the primary/only means of avoiding damage. Yi's dash does have iframes, but it starts out decidedly ground-only and never stops having a short but very noticeable recharge. Instead the game asks you to parry nearly everything, which sounds like a tall order. Fortunately this narrowing og choices allowed Red Candle to build the game around parries; ensuring enemies always telegraph their attacks, allowing mistimed parries to still convert damage to 'internal' damage and making the act of parrying feel incredibly satisfying.

But you won't just be passively standing around as the enemy whales on you; parrying is also your offence and finding windows for regular attacks is a key part of defeating many bosses. A successful parry (perfect or otherwise) grants a Qi charge, allowing you to dash through an enemy while attaching a talisman. Hold the same button for a beat and the talisman explodes, dealing a solid chunk of damage (plus whatever internal damage you managed to inflict). Combat revolves around this parry/talisman dance, especially once you gain the ability to channel for longer and expend morre Qi charges for bigger damage. Further amplified once you find a jade dealing internal damage to enemies on perfect parries... you get the idea. It's nice to see a dev commit so wholeheartedly to making one style of combat feel amazing and pull it off.

Regular enemies can definitely pose a challenge, especially in groups until you get/upgrade your Azure Bow, but the titular 9 Sols are the main draw here. Each of them has their own biome, theme and unique combat wrinkle, in addition to providing you with plenty of reason to parry them into oblivion. The difficulty curve is steep but smooth; with bosses often introducing new concepts which build to a truly incredible climax in the true ending. You'll die a bunch; but once you start to learn the rhythm of the fight it feels amazing, and you'll feel like you deserve every victory.

This whole review has focused on combat because it's the star of the show, but it's not the only good thing about the game. Platforming and movement in general are smooth and satisfying, secrets strike a good balance between challenging and ridiculously obscure and you'll grow to love the characters if you go out of your way to interact with them. I highly recommend getting the true ending at some point for peak boss design followed by an emotional finale to your journey. Definitely give this a try if you're a souls-like or Metroidvania fan, and if you like both (that's me!) it'll probably be one of your favourite games.
